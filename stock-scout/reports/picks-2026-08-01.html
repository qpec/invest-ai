How to read this page

Two columns, deliberately not one number

The score is an absolute 100-point card — points against fixed lines the owner declared, not a ranking against whatever else happened to be listed. Where a metric could not be computed the card shrinks its own denominator rather than scoring a zero, which is why the points column shows what was actually available and an evidence tier beside it.

The verdict is a separate count of seven fragility probes over the price and cash record. It adds no points and suppresses nothing on its own — it names the failure mode and leaves the decision to you. Severities are counted, never averaged, because an average lets a good probe cancel a fatal one.

A business can be Exceptional and Fragile at once. That pairing has its own section below, and it is the most useful thing on this page.

Before you act on any of this

What this page cannot see

It reads what already happened to the cash and the price. Lawsuits, regulation, a competitor's roadmap, a fraud not yet in the numbers, key-person risk — none of it is here, and no probe returning "nothing found" is a statement that nothing is there.

An unmeasured probe is not a passed one. Where the filer never tagged the figure, the layer says so. Customer concentration is the sharpest case: only about a tenth of these filers tag it at all, and the median disclosure is years old.

The card's percentage is of what could be measured. Two names with the same percentage can rest on very different amounts of evidence — that is what the tier beside each score is for, and why this page sorts on it first.

None of this has been validated as a strategy. The scorecard's anchors and the fragility lines are declared and reasoned, not walk-forward tested. The only rules in this repo that survived a blind walk-forward are the v3 formation's entry and exit rules, and they are a different question from this page.
