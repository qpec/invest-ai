The core idea · start here

What is a thesis?

A thesis is a short, written argument for why you own something — and, just as important, the specific things that would prove you wrong. It turns "I have a good feeling about this stock" into "I own this for these reasons, and I'll admit I'm wrong if any of them happen." The system never watches a stock. It watches its thesis.

◆
Think of it as a bet with the reasoning written down beforehand, plus a pre-agreed list of "if this happens, I was wrong." Writing the exit conditions before you're attached is the whole trick: when bad news arrives, you're not arguing with yourself — you're calmly checking a list you already made.
Anatomy of a thesis — what gets written down

▤ The business, in 2 sentences

What it does and how it makes money — from memory, no spreadsheet. Can't say it in two sentences? You don't understand it well enough to own it.

⛨ The moat

The durable advantage that keeps rivals out — customer lock-in, network effects, a trusted brand, a cost edge, or regulation. Backed by evidence, not hope.

€ Owner earnings

How much real, spendable cash the business throws off. The number that actually matters — explained just below.

◷ A fair price

The price range you'd consider reasonable to pay — decided in advance, so a market panic can't talk you into overpaying.

▲ Conviction & horizon

How sure you are — high / medium / low, which caps your position size — and how long you'll hold. The default is "forever."

◎ 2–5 invalidation triggers

The falsifiable "I was wrong if…" conditions — at least one tied to the moat. These are exactly what the system monitors.

✍︎
Plus a 10-year statement in your own words: "Would I still hold this if the market closed for a decade — and why?" When a trigger later fires, the system quotes this paragraph back to you, so you weigh the news against your own considered conviction, not a flash of fear.

The link between intact and under_review goes both ways: if you refute the alarm with evidence, the thesis returns to intact and the trigger re-arms. broken is a dead end — a fresh position later needs a brand-new thesis, run through the Gate again.

⌘ At birth — the Gate

Before it can go live, a candidate must survive the five-step Gate and you must commit 2–5 checkable triggers. A thesis with no way to be proven wrong isn't allowed to exist.

◉ Continuously — the Watchdog

Every week, and on every event, each trigger is re-tested against fresh data. Two guarantees: a trigger can only fire on fresh, complete data (stale data reads as "can't see," never a pass), and only a pre-committed trigger can ever raise an alarm.

⟳ Yearly — your judgment

Once a year you re-affirm conviction, trust in management, and that it's still in your circle of competence. The numbers refresh weekly; your judgment refreshes yearly — so neither goes stale.

↻ Catch-up

After downtime, Persistent=true fires one coalesced run. A per-run-type sweep additionally re-runs any day that crashed mid-execution — so the letter is never skipped, at worst late.

♥ Dead-man

The missing morning letter is the primary alarm. A content-free heartbeat ping also fires after every successful daily run (~26h window) — enabled from day one as the system's one external touchpoint, covering box-death even during a quiet-mode vacation.

✉ Delivery

Jobs never call Telegram directly — they render, archive, and enqueue into a durable outbox in one transaction. A separate daemon delivers. Alerts survive any outage.

⏸
Pause mode. Declare an absence window and every deadline and skip counter freezes — by arithmetic, not by mutating anything. Alerts still deliver; weekly reviews still run; but a vacation never mints a fake “ignored” decision into the record.

Triggers · what could break a thesis, in plain words

The warning signs it watches for.

Each thesis commits 2–5 specific, checkable warning signs — the things that, if they happen, mean the reason you bought is in doubt. Four are checked automatically from the company's financial filings; the fifth is a question you answer. Here's what each one watches, in everyday terms — the formulas are one click away, only if you want them.

Two ideas worth understanding — in plain words
💶
Owner earningsThe real, spendable cash a business could actually hand you at year-end — after paying to keep the lights on and to stay competitive. Not the "profit" on paper, which can be dressed up. This is the number that matters.
Picture a rental flat you own. Owner earnings is the rent left in your pocket after the mortgage, the repairs, and repainting to keep it rentable — the cash you can actually spend, not the headline rent.
Show the math
owner_fcf_ttm = fcf_ttm − sbc_ttm fcf_ttm = Σ last 4 quarters (OCF − |CapEx|)
Stock-based comp (SBC) is subtracted because paying staff in shares is a real cost of staying competitive — large for software firms. TTM = trailing twelve months.
🏷️
The price tag — measured in yearsHow expensive the business is, expressed as how many years of today's owner-cash you're paying for it. You decide a fair range in advance; a price below it — while the business is healthy — is "on sale."
If a business throws off €10 of owner-cash per share and trades at €200, you're paying 20× — about twenty years' worth. Say your fair band is 18–24×. If it falls to 14× with nothing actually wrong, that's the buy-more signal: a wonderful business on sale.
Show the math
multiple = price / owner_fcf_per_share fair_band = [ low× , high× ] (you set it) "on sale" = price ≤ (1 − 20%) × band_mid × cash/share fair entry = price ≤ band_high × cash/share
One measure only — no P/E, no P/S. The multiple you consider fair is fixed; the cash-per-share refreshes every week, so the threshold never goes stale.
⚠
Price is never a trigger — unconditional. No decline trigger, no "euphoria" trigger. Price appears only in the buy-opportunity and fair-entry checks. A drawdown on an intact thesis is an opportunity, framed against your own anchor; it is never an alarm.

◈ Hidden concentration (FR12)

Twelve positions can secretly be three bets. Each weekly review checks this with real math, not gut feel.

If your 12 holdings all tend to rise and fall together, you don't really own 12 bets — you own a few, repeated. N_eff is "how many independent bets you actually hold." Below 4, the book is far more concentrated than the position count suggests.
Show the math
daily returns · local currency · 252-day lookback group holdings when they move together (correlation > 0.70) N_eff = 1 / Σ (group weight)² (floor 4.0)
Local currency, so a shared EUR/USD swing can't fake-merge every US holding into one cluster. The weekly review prints the groups, their weights, N_eff, and the full correlation grid.
⚡
Leverage tripwire — continuous. Any position with leverage > 1 or a CFD fires an immediate Hell-No violation alert. The leverage veto is enforced not just at the Gate, but on every snapshot.
Validation · where validity is ensured

Defense in depth — from the wire to the constitution.

Validity is not a single check; it is four layers, each independent of the one below. A value that fails any layer is suspended and reported as suspended — it can never silently pass as green.

Layer 1 Data validation — empty is failure

The hardened data layer refuses to trust silence. Yahoo returns an empty (0,0) frame with no exception for missing fundamentals — so the adapter treats it as a fetch failure, not a zero.

  • Empty / None / zero-row frames, NaN or non-positive prices → fetch failure: keep last-known-good, stamp STALE with age, never write zeros.
  • Statement sanity before any append: plausible row count, recent period-end, the pinned rows present.
  • Every fetched value carries a freshness stamp that travels with it into every downstream computation.

Layer 2 The freshness gate — STALE can never PASS or FIRE

A check can only fire on fresh, complete data. Anything else returns an honest non-verdict.

  • STALE — stale/empty inputs · BOOTSTRAPPING — not enough history yet (prints the date it becomes evaluable) · UNVERIFIABLE — a prompted question skipped.
  • None of these can ever read as PASS or FIRE. Suspended checks are reported as suspended, never passed.
  • Three consecutive unverifiable weeks escalate to the weekly headline — silence is surfaced, not buried.

Layer 3 Structural invariants — the constitution, enforced by the database

The most load-bearing rules aren't conventions the code politely follows — they're physically impossible to break.

  • Append-only / versioned — SQLite triggers RAISE(ABORT) on any UPDATE/DELETE of a protected row. History can't be rewritten.
  • Cost-basis wall — advice code reads only a SQL view that omits avg_open_price; the field cannot reach any recommendation.
  • Benchmark quarantine — the benchmark lives in a separate database file the daily/weekly/event code never opens. An absent input, not a suppressed section.
  • Journaled decisions — a config change is a foreign-key constraint away from a journal entry: an unjournaled change is a database error.

Layer 4 Build-time proof — determinism & golden tests

No LLM runs in the scheduled runtime, so every output is deterministic — which makes it provable.

  • Byte-exact golden files pin every letter, alert, and report variant to its specified wording and calm register.
  • An import-graph test proves the benchmark and screener are reachable only from their one sanctioned module.
  • A fail-closed license gate checks every dependency against a permissive-only allowlist before the system may run.

Architecture · the shape of the system

How a run becomes a message.

Two SQLite files, a render layer that produces two skins from one context, a durable outbox, and a hand-rolled daemon that is the one interface you speak through. The benchmark file is physically unreachable from the daily path.

stdlib spine

Scheduling, supervision, storage, Telegram, git — all standard library + systemd. Four pip packages, all analysis-only.

no LLM at runtime

Every scheduled output is a deterministic template. LLMs help you author theses at the desk, never in the loop.

local-only

Data never leaves the box. The only outbound calls are Yahoo, Telegram, and a content-free heartbeat.

10-year posture

Pinned interpreter + on-box wheelhouse, a quarterly upgrade ritual, and a yfinance emergency lane.

Decision Journal · The Study · Quarterly honesty

How it keeps you honest — and keeps upgrading.

Every decision is recorded with its reasoning at the moment, then graded later on process, not price. Once a quarter it asks the only question that matters. And every week it feeds your own judgment — the compounding asset behind all the others.

Grades are filled only at review, judged against what you said you expected and what would prove you wrong — never against the raw price move.

↑ The Study — the Naval loop

A weekly digest, capped at one screen, that sharpens the owner's judgment — the leverage behind every other decision.

  • One holding restudied (rotation — each name ~every 10–15 weeks)
  • One mental-model prompt on the live portfolio — may propose a new trigger
  • Journal items approaching review
  • A 20-minute reading from the restudied name
  • A one-line circle note — did the circle grow or shrink?

Never contains performance numbers, price echoes, or new-idea generation.

◉ The quarterly honesty check

The one place a benchmark, P/L, or cost basis ever appears — quarantined here to starve envy.

“Would an index fund have beaten my process?”

Portfolio (EUR) vs S&P 500 Total Return in EUR — the honest passive counterfactual given the owner's tax residency, where UCITS trackers are punitively taxed. Time-weighted, so deposits never look like alpha; labelled indicative, not authoritative.

⚖︎
Standing exit clause — “if a clean process is persistently behind the index over 36 months, the honest conclusion changes to indexing.” Being behind triggers process questions, never “trade more.”
Guardrails · deliberately absent

What the system refuses to do.

Every exclusion traces to a failure mode in the Buffett / Munger / Naval constitution. The discipline is the point.

✓
"No action needed" is the celebrated default. Most days the system sends one calm message and expects nothing back. Doing nothing is a first-class, positive outcome — the design is built against action bias.
✕
No price triggers. A price drop on an intact thesis is an opportunity, not an alarm.
✕
No open-ended news scanning. Only pre-committed triggers can raise an alarm.
✕
No autonomous idea generation. Screening is human-triggered only; every candidate still passes the Gate.
✕
No P/L, portfolio value, or cost basis in the daily letter. Cash appears as a band-% only; cost basis is forbidden as an input to advice.
✕
No streak counters or self-benchmarking. The index comparison lives only in the quarterly honesty check — quarantined from daily view to starve envy.
✕
No ML stock-picking, no exotic derivatives, no leverage. A leverage position trips an immediate Hell-No alert, continuously.
